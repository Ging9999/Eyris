# Applying 2024-26 LLM-agent trading research

Generated by `python scripts/agentic_research.py`. The runtime agent remains LLM-free and deterministic,
as specified. The research was applied in the two ways that are compatible with that constraint and testable without
look-ahead bias.

## What the recent agent literature says

| Paper | Finding | Implication for Eyris |
|---|---|---|
| [StockBench (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02209) | Most LLM agents fail to beat buy-and-hold on return or risk-adjusted return over multi-month live-like tests | A disciplined passive-ish core is a strong baseline; many rival teams will trail it |
| [FINSABER (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07078) | Reported LLM advantages vanish over 20 years and 100+ symbols. Agents are too conservative in bull markets and too aggressive in bear markets. The authors recommend regime-aware risk control | Modelled as a competitor; trend (regime) control re-tested |
| [LLM agents in production (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05663) | 7.5M calls over 6 months: volatility-blind sizing, no directional edge, and the operating/risk layer drives behaviour more than the model | Our edge must come from the risk/execution layer, which is where Eyris already is |
| [Agent Market Arena (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11695), [LiveTradeBench (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03628) | Live tests: agent architecture dominates; higher general-benchmark scores do not mean better trading | Same |
| [Look-Ahead-Bench (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13770) | General LLMs leak future knowledge into financial backtests | A runtime LLM cannot be validated honestly on 2021-25 data, so it is excluded |
| [AlphaAgent (KDD 2025)](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2502.16789), R&D-Agent-Quant, [FaVOR (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30192) | LLMs propose factor hypotheses offline; empirical validation and decay control decide | Applied as step 1 below |
| [TradingAgents (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20138), [FinCon (NeurIPS 2024)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/f7ae4fe91d96f50abc2211f09b6a7e49-Paper-Conference.pdf) | Multi-agent analyst/risk-manager hierarchies | Their risk-manager role maps to our deterministic exposure/band layer |

## 1. Agentic factor mining (offline, AlphaAgent-style)

An LLM (this assistant) proposed 11 literature-grounded factor hypotheses (`eyris/factors.py`). Each is computed from completed days only.
Each was tested as daily rank IC against the next 5-day return. The keep rule was |t| ≥ 2.5 on 2021-24 (non-overlapping), with the sign
confirmed on 2025 validation at t ≥ 1.

| Factor | Hypothesis | Source | IC train | t train | IC val | t val | Kept |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| bab | low-beta stocks earn more per unit risk (sign: -beta, 20d) | Frazzini & Pedersen 2014 | -0.0014 | -0.05 | -0.0562 | -0.94 | no |
| ivol | high idiosyncratic volatility underperforms (sign: -ivol) | Ang, Hodrick, Xing & Zhang 2006 | -0.0194 | -1.08 | +0.0087 | +0.25 | no |
| max | lottery-like max daily return underperforms (sign: -max) | Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw 2011 | -0.0151 | -0.74 | -0.0154 | -0.31 | no |

| resid_mom | residual (beta-adjusted) momentum is cleaner | Blitz, Huij & Martens 2011 | +0.0312 | +1.55 | -0.0064 | -0.14 | no |

**No factor survived.** The best train t-stat is 1.6, and most signs flip in 2025. That is the alpha-decay pattern these papers
warn about, made worse by a 30-stock cross-section. The factor tilt (step 3) therefore never ran, and nothing was added to the live agent.

## 2. Competitor field modelled on documented LLM-agent behaviour

The competition encourages LLM agents, and the score is a rank against other teams. So a third reference field (14 including us) now
mimics the documented behaviour: the starter-kit momentum agent, a daily news chaser (yesterday's top 5), volatility-blind
top-3 at 30% each, an hourly overtrader, a FINSABER-style regime contrarian, noisy daily pickers, a diversifier, and a
team that misses rounds (cash). The high-turnover members lose 1-13% per 15 days, mostly to fees, as the papers report.

Re-selection over the same grid plus trend (regime) control, scored as the mean median-rank percentile across the full, active and
LLM fields:

| Period | Current config: median rank vs LLM-style field |
|---|---:|
| Train 2021-24 | 3.25 / 14 |
| Validation 2025-01..09 | 3.25 / 14 |
| Holdout 2025-Q4 (indicative) | 2.75 / 14 |

- **The current configuration is still the best** (tied at the top). Trend control and other exposure levels did not beat it.
- Against the LLM-style field alone, 50-85% exposure is a statistical tie in every period. 100% is consistently worse.
  So if you believe the field will be mostly LLM agents, raising `gross` to 0.7 costs little rank and earns more return,
  at the cost of a larger worst-case drawdown (11.1% vs 7.9% in validation).

## Why not add a runtime LLM (news / analyst agent)?

The competition allows it. There are four reasons not to:
1. It breaks your no-LLM, deterministic constraint.
2. It cannot be backtested honestly, because the models have read about 2021-25 (Look-Ahead-Bench).
3. The benchmarks above show no reliable edge.
4. Disclosure and reproducibility review are harder.

If you want to try one anyway, the safest role suggested by FinCon/TradingAgents is a *risk veto*. For example, it would trim a
name ahead of an earnings call or major news, and could only reduce exposure. It would need your sign-off on the constraint first.
