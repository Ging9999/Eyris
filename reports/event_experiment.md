# Earnings-event risk control: experiment

Generated by `python scripts/event_experiment.py`. The Official window (Oct 12-30, 2026) is Q3 earnings season: 24 of the 30 stocks
report inside it (see `data/earnings_dates.csv`). The rule exits (or halves) a stock at 15:30 the day before its earnings price jump.
Optionally it restores the stock after the open; otherwise the normal no-trade band brings it back.

**Calendar check:** on mapped jump days the abnormal opening gap averages 3.8%, against 0.6% on other days (a placebo one day earlier: 0.7%).
So the timing of each announcement (pre-open vs after-close) is mapped correctly.

Primary test: earnings-season windows (≥ 15 announcements in the 15 days). Score: mean median-rank percentile across the
full, active and LLM-style competitor fields.

## Verdict: not adopted (switched off by default)

- 2025 validation looked strong: the score went from 0.156 to 0.120, with a higher median return and lower drawdown.
  But 2021-24 earnings seasons reversed the rank result (0.190 to 0.205), and Q4 2025 was flat. The 2025 return boost was year-specific.
- **What is consistent:** lower drawdown in every period. In 2021-24 earnings seasons the median MDD fell from 1.41% to 1.29% and the
  worst from 5.96% to 5.41%. The extra trading costs about as much turnover rank as the drawdown rank gains.
- To prioritise crash protection over expected rank, set in `models/params.json`:
  `"event_mode": "trim", "event_cut": 1.0, "event_min_move": 0.03`. The live runner then refreshes the earnings calendar
  each day and logs `event_trims` in every `decision_log.json`.
