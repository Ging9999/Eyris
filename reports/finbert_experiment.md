# FinBERT headline sentiment (FNSPID, 2022-23)

Generated by `python scripts/fnspid_extract.py` then `python scripts/finbert_experiment.py` (2026-10-03, ~32 min, mostly
CPU scoring); raw results in `finbert_experiment.json`. Model: `ProsusAI/finbert`, revision pinned in `eyris/finbert.py`.

**Verdict: no edge. FinBERT stays shadow-only (logged live, never used in decisions).**

## Why FinBERT

FinBERT is open-source and allowed (organizer clarification, Oct 2026). It was trained on financial text from before 2020, so
unlike a modern LLM it has not seen the 2021-25 news and can be backtested honestly. It is deterministic on CPU.

## Data

FNSPID (free, Hugging Face): the 2016-2023 news is in `nasdaq_exteral_data.csv` (23 GB, sorted by symbol). The extractor
binary-searches it with HTTP Range requests and fetches only our stocks' slices (3.4 GB), keeping 91,124 headlines from
Dec 2020 on. Coverage is uneven:
- each stock is capped at about 8,700 headlines, so busy names start late (AAPL 2022-06, MSFT 2022-04, TSLA 2022-05,
  AMZN 2023-03);
- BAC, GOOGL, META, JNJ, JPM, UNH, PFE and WMT are absent or nearly so (LLY has 7 rows);
- timestamps are unreliable (date-only stamps, and times that contradict the headline).

## Design (pre-registered; the date split was revised from coverage only, before any result)

- **No lookahead:** a headline dated X is used only from the first trading day after X. The signal for day d is each stock's
  mean FinBERT net sentiment, P(pos) - P(neg), over headlines first usable that day. Stocks without news score 0.
- **Split:** selection = windows inside 2022-04 .. 2023-03; confirmation = windows inside 2023-04 .. 2023-12.
  The original 2021-22 / 2023 plan would have compared mostly news-free periods.
- **Variants:** reduce-only trims at round 1 (threshold -0.3 / -0.5 x cut 50% / 100%, needs >= 2 headlines, restored when
  the signal clears), and tilts w ~ invvol x exp(k x signal), k = 0.25 / 0.5.
- **Adoption bar:** beat current on selection AND not lose on confirmation. Even a pass could not be checked on 2025.

## Results

Predictive check: daily cross-sectional Spearman IC vs the next-day return.

| Half | Days | Names with news | IC | t | Negative spikes | Next-day excess return | t |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Selection | 251 | 17.9 | +0.037 | 2.2 | 57 | +9.2 bps | 0.4 |
| Confirmation | 188 | 19.0 | -0.001 | -0.0 | 22 | +5.1 bps | 0.2 |

Backtest (selection score: mean median-rank percentile vs the three fields; lower is better):

| Variant | Selection (237 windows) | Confirmation (165 windows) | Median turnover |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **current** | **0.218** | **0.200** | 0.0048 |
| tilt k=0.25 | 0.221 | 0.200 | 0.0048 |
| tilt k=0.5 | 0.221 | 0.200 | 0.0049 |
| trim -0.5, cut 50% | 0.226 | 0.200 | 0.0052 |
| trim -0.5, cut 100% | 0.229 | 0.202 | 0.0057 |
| trim -0.3, cut 50% | 0.229 | 0.209 | 0.0068 |
| trim -0.3, cut 100% | 0.250 | 0.223 | 0.0090 |

- **The weak positive IC in the first half did not survive the second half.** This is the same decay seen for every other signal.
- **Negative-news stocks did slightly *better* than the market the next day.** By the time the news is usable, it is priced in.
- **Every trim loses, through turnover, with no drawdown benefit. The tilts tie at best.** None passed.

## Implications

- FinBERT shadow mode (`finbert_shadow: true`) keeps logging live sentiment to `private/<round>/finbert_log.json` after each
  round. It has no effect on decisions and builds a forward record.
- Trimming on negative news after publication cost rank here, consistent with the rejected earnings trims. This is evidence
  for switching the reduce-only Opus news veto **off** for Official, though not a direct test of it: Opus judges materiality
  and the live feed is timelier. The pre-registered rule in `eyris/review.py:DECISION_RULES` still decides.
